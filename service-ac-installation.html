Cooling · High SEER

Cooling that actually reaches the bedrooms

Edmonton summers are short and sharp. An oversized condenser short-cycles and leaves the upstairs sticky; an undersized one runs until midnight and still cannot catch up. We size the coil and condenser together and check the furnace blower can move the air.

The pad, whip, disconnect, and line-set are the unglamorous half of a good install. We recover the old refrigerant, replace undersized or contaminated copper, weigh in the charge, and confirm superheat and subcooling — not a 'close enough' gauge glance.

Questions we actually get

When should I install AC in Edmonton?

April through June is kinder than the first heat wave. We still install in July — you just wait in a warm house if the city is slammed.

Can you add AC to my existing furnace?

Usually, if the blower and ducts can handle the coil. We check that before we sell a condenser.

How long does it take?

A straightforward add-on is often a day. New electrical or a coil that needs plenum work can take longer.

Heat pump instead of AC?

If you also want winter efficiency, yes. See the heat-pump page. If you only want July cooling, a straight AC is simpler.
