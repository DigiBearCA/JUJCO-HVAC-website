Home/Services/Heat Pump Installation

One outdoor unit for winter heat and July cooling, with your furnace kept as dual-fuel backup when the design day demands it.

Heat & cool · Cold climate

Heat pumps that still work in Alberta

A builder-grade heat pump that quits at −10°C is the wrong machine for this city. We spec cold-climate equipment with a published capacity at Edmonton design temperatures, then decide whether it should stand alone or sit in dual-fuel with the furnace you already trust.

The outdoor pad, line-set, electrical, and defrost settings are where installs go cheap and then fail. We run new line-set when the old one is the wrong size or full of residue, set backup-heat lockouts so the furnace does not fight the heat pump, and show you which thermostat mode to leave it in from October to April.

Questions we actually get

Will a heat pump heat my house at −30°C?

A true cold-climate unit still produces heat there. Output drops, which is why dual-fuel with a furnace is often the right Edmonton answer. We do not sell a unit that needs emergency heat every January night.

Do I keep my furnace?

Often yes. Dual-fuel uses the heat pump most of the winter and brings the furnace in on the worst days. If the furnace is finished, we talk about a full system, not a leftover box.

Is it noisy outside?

Modern cold-climate units are quieter than a tired 1990s condenser. Placement and a level pad matter more than the brochure decibel number.

What about ice on the outdoor unit?

Defrost is normal. We set it so the unit clears itself without turning your sidewalk into a rink or short-cycling all night.
